## What the supervisor source shows

- A tick is one-shot. launchd runs it every `[project].interval` seconds (`StartInterval`, 300 in `fleet.toml`); each tick runs `ci_watch`, `flo facts`, the limits collector, then launches detached steps and exits.
- Ticks cannot overlap: `tick()` takes a `mkdir` lock (`tick.lock`) and returns "another tick is running" when it is held (stale after 1200 s). A step already running is not launched again: `decide()` returns nothing while `s["running"]` or `ctx["busy"]` holds, and `launch()` records each step.
- So a faster trigger does not by itself cause duplicate launches; the lock and the busy check do that work. A test must still prove it (E23-S11 covers the lock; this story adds the burst case).
- launchd offers `WatchPaths`, which starts the job when a watched path changes, alongside `StartInterval` as the backstop. I could not test launchd here (no macOS), so its behaviour is unverified.

## Design

1. **Wake file.** `author.sh`, `review.sh`, `submit.sh` and `finish.sh` write `.scratch/wake` (touch it) when a step ends, after the `exit N` line is in the log.
2. **Supervisor.** `fleet.toml` gets an optional `[project].watch` list of runner-relative paths. `install()` adds `WatchPaths` for them to the plist. `StartInterval` stays as the backstop. A tick that finds the lock held exits as it does today.
3. **Throttle.** Keep launchd's default throttle (the job will not restart more than about once per ten seconds); do not lower it.
