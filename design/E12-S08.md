## Requirements (inlined)

- **SRC-012** — Awards may be split by line or quantity across vendors.
- **SRC-013** — An award must record selected vendor, awarded lines, amount, rationale, evaluator results, exceptions, and approval history.
- **SRC-014** — A non-lowest-price award must require an explicit justification when configured.
- **SRC-016** — Award totals must not exceed approved requisition scope or available reserved funds without an approved amendment.
- **REQ-014** — Split sourcing and partial awards must be supported without exceeding approved quantity or amount.
- **FIN-011** — a release happens exactly once.

## Rules

- **Split (SRC-012, REQ-014):** a line may go to several vendors by quantity. For each RFQ line,
  Σ award_line.quantity ≤ rfq_line.quantity (422 per line). A vendor must have quoted that line.
- **Non-lowest (SRC-014):** `not_lowest` is set by the server against E12-S06's per-line lowest.
  When the org setting `sourcing.require_non_lowest_justification` (new, bool, default **true**) is
  on, every `not_lowest` line needs a justification (1..500) at submit, or 422 per line.
- **Ties (decision 115):** where several vendors tie at E12-S06's per-line lowest, a line awarded to
  any tied vendor other than the earliest `sent_at` (E12-S06's tie order, `comparison.py:198`) needs `tie_override_reason` (1..500) at
  submit, or 422 per line. The earliest tied vendor needs none.
- **Budget amount (decision 211):** an award line's *budget amount* is its price, plus its tax when
  the org setting `tax.consumes_budget` is on for the RFQ's unit, plus its freight share when
  `freight.consumes_budget` is on (both new settings in `modules/org/settings.py`, bool, default
  **true**, resolved through the nearest-unit override). The scope and funds checks and the release
  below use budget amounts; the comparison (E12-S06) still shows full landed cost.
- **Scope and funds (SRC-016),** checked at submit **and** again at approval, under the locks:
  for each requisition on the award, `award amount for its lines` (Σ budget amounts, in the RFQ currency) must not exceed
  either **(a) scope**, the approved amount for those lines pro rata by quantity (E12-S06's formula),
  or **(b) funds**, the live reservation remaining on its sub-project. Over either → 409
  `ErrorCode.CONFLICT` (not `INSUFFICIENT_BUDGET`; the taxonomy is closed) with `checks` = `{problem: "over_scope", requisition, scope, funds, award, over_scope_by, over_funds_by}`,
  each `over_*_by` = `max(0, award − scope)` and `max(0, award − funds)` respectively. The way out is to
  change the selection or amend the requisition (pages/sourcing.md §2). Nothing is disabled.
- **Submit** (`rfq.approve` on the unit; the RFQ must be `under_evaluation`):
  1. `require_workflow_for(conn, scope, "award", award_id, "awards")`, or 409 `workflow_not_set_up`. **Never auto-approve** (decision 204).
  2. `assert_current_upstream` for every requisition (a stale version, a pending amendment or expired codes → 409).
  3. The checks above.
  4. Status `approval_pending`, and start the approval.
  Register the `award` `DocumentAdapter` (`approve_permission = "rfq.approve"`, target the RFQ's unit).
- **On approval** (`on_outcome`, **inside the decision transaction**; lock order: every
  `project_balance` involved in id order, then the requisitions in id order, then the award):
  1. Re-run the scope and funds check (409 rolls back the decision).
  2. `requisitions.service.award_lines(conn, scope, [(requisition_line_id, qty)], actor)`: `awarded_qty += qty` (the CHECK `awarded_qty ≤ quantity` is the backstop).
  3. **Release and return (decision 205),** per requisition: `unused = scope of the awarded lines − award amount` (≥ 0). If `unused > 0`, call a new `requisitions.service.release_unawarded(conn, scope, requisition_id, unused, award_id, actor)`. It calls `funding.reduce_to(new_amount = remaining − unused, version = award.version, source = f"award:{award_id}", see ## Decisions)` and then transfers the released amount **sub-project → main project** with `post_legs` (idempotency key `award:{award_id}:return:{requisition_id}`). Exactly once: a retried decision posts nothing new (FIN-011).
  4. Award `approved`; RFQ `awarded`.
  5. **Requisition status (decision 206):** if every non-cancelled line now has `awarded_qty = quantity − cancelled_qty`, move it `sourced → awarded → open_for_purchase` in this transaction (two audited transitions) via `requisitions.service.mark_awarded`.
- **On rejection or return:** `Outcome.REJECTED` (either `reject_mode`) and `Outcome.RETURNED` both move the award `approval_pending → draft` (the award vocabulary has no `rejected`; the approval history keeps the decision). Nothing is posted.
- **On withdrawal** (`Outcome.WITHDRAWN`, from either withdraw path): the adapter's `on_outcome` first requires `context.permitted("rfq.edit", <the award snapshot's target>)`, or 403 (rolls the withdrawal back), as `requisitions/approval.py:162-171` does; then `approval_pending → draft`. Nothing is posted.
- **Standalone requisitions:** at submit, a requisition on the award with no generated sub-project (a standalone request whose approval created a new project: no project has `generated_by_requisition_id` = the requisition) → 422 `ErrorCode.VALIDATION_FAILED` with `checks = {problem: "standalone_not_supported", requisition}`. Nothing is written.
- Register the `award` link-graph node (with `access`).

## API (`/api/v1/sourcing/rfqs/{id}/award`)

`POST /` (draft from a basis: `{basis}` copies E12-S06's selection; `manual` starts empty),
`PUT /lines`, `PATCH /` (rationale), `GET /` (with live checks and figures), `POST /submit`, and

## Acceptance

- [ ] Migrations up, down and data preservation.
- [ ] Split: one line to two vendors by quantity; over the RFQ quantity → 422.
- [ ] Justification: required per not-lowest line when the setting is on; not required when off.
- [ ] No published award workflow → 409 on submit; nothing written.
- [ ] Over scope and over funds each → 409 with the figures.
- [ ] **Approval posts:** a hand-computed example where the award is 900 against a scope of 1,000. The ledger shows one release of 100 on the sub-project and one transfer of 100 sub → main; the sub-project reservation remaining is reduced by 100; **drift is empty** (`budget/reconcile`). Approve twice (a retried decision) → no second release or transfer.
- [ ] **Concurrency (real Postgres):** one requisition line on two RFQs (`rfq_line` is unique per
  RFQ only), each RFQ with its own award for that line's full quantity; both approved at once → exactly one succeeds (the other fails the awarded-quantity check under the lock), and the ledger has one release.
- [ ] A requisition fully awarded → `open_for_purchase` (via `awarded`); a partly awarded one stays `sourced`.
- [ ] Rejection → draft, and no ledger entry. A `return` decision (`Outcome.RETURNED`) → draft, and no ledger entry.
- [ ] Withdraw through the generic `POST /api/v1/approvals/instances/{id}/withdraw` by a user without `rfq.edit` on the RFQ's unit → 403, the instance still `running`, the award still `approval_pending`.
- [ ] An award with a line from a standalone requisition that has no sub-project → 422 `standalone_not_supported` on submit; nothing written.
- [ ] Partial award: tax and freight prorated by awarded/quoted quantity; a hand-computed example matches to the cent.
- [ ] Withdraw while `approval_pending`: the approval ends `WITHDRAWN`, the award is `draft`, no ledger entry; withdraw again → 409.
- [ ] Re-submit after rejection releases with a new version key; a retried approval does not release twice.
- [ ] TEN-010 and authorization per role (a hidden Submit is refused by the server).
- [ ] `flo check` green.

## Decisions (operator, 27 Oct 2026; settled from pre-flight, were B1-B3)

- **Authorization:** `POST /`, `PUT /lines`, `PATCH /` and `POST /withdraw` need `rfq.edit` on the
  RFQ's unit; `GET /` needs `rfq.view`; `POST /submit` stays `rfq.approve`. The award
  `DocumentAdapter` has `read_permission = "rfq.view"` and `accepts_project_assignment = False`
  (the award inherits the RFQ's project). A refused call is 403 for a user who can see the RFQ and
  404 for another tenant.
- **Partial awards (money):** tax and freight share are **prorated** by `awarded quantity / quoted
  quantity`, quantized once in the vendor currency through `kernel/money.py` (never summed from
  rounded parts). `amount_vendor`, the scope and funds checks and the released amount all use the
  prorated figures. E12-S06's comparison prices the full quoted quantity (`comparison.py:212,229`)
  and is not changed here: label its figure `Full quote` and record "make the comparison prorate on
  partial selection" in `notes.followup`.
- **Exactly-once release (money):** `funding.reduce_to(version=award.version, source=f"award:{award_id}")`. `version` is bumped on
  every submit, so a retried approval reuses its key (a no-op) and a re-submit after rejection gets a
  new key. Do not use the requisition version: other reductions in the funding round share it.
  **Key collision (Opus pre-flight, 27 Oct 2026):** `reduce_to` keys the release
  `req:{requisition_id}:release:{funding_round}:reduce:{version}` (`requisitions/funding.py:155`), so two
  awards on two RFQs that share one requisition (split sourcing, REQ-014) and reach the same award
  version would share a key: the second release is swallowed as a retry while its transfer back still
  posts, and drift appears. Add a keyword-only `source: str | None = None` to `reduce_to`; when given,
  the key is `req:{requisition_id}:release:{funding_round}:reduce:{source}:{version}`, and when `None`
  the key is unchanged (existing callers and tests keep theirs). Test: two partial awards on two RFQs
  for one requisition, both at award version 2, both approved one after the other → two releases and
  two transfers, drift empty.
- **Withdrawal:** one transaction: lock the running approval instance, record the withdrawal decision
  and end it `WITHDRAWN` through `approvals.service.withdraw` (as `decisions.py:350` does), then move
  the award `approval_pending → draft`. A withdrawn award releases nothing and writes no ledger entry.

- **Planner defaults (27 Oct 2026, operator asked for defaults; from the second pre-flight):**
  (a) `Outcome.RETURNED` is handled like rejection: `approval_pending → draft`, no ledger entry.
  (b) Standalone requisitions without a sub-project are refused at submit (422
  `standalone_not_supported`); the release path for them is unspecified money, so record
  "award release for a standalone requisition (reservation on its own project, no sub-project)" in
  `notes.followup`. (c) The generic approvals withdraw endpoint authorizes with the adapter's
  `read_permission` (`api/approvals.py:101-105`) and the requester check
  (`decisions.py:412`); the award adapter adds the `rfq.edit` check on `WITHDRAWN` above, so a
  viewer cannot withdraw an award through it.

## Plants

1. Release before locking the balance: the concurrency test sees two releases.
2. Skip the transfer back: the "returned to the main project" assertion fails.
3. Auto-approve when no workflow is published: the 409 test fails.
4. Drop the `rfq.edit` check on `WITHDRAWN`: the generic-endpoint viewer test fails.
