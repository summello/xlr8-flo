# E12-S24 — Lines with no priced quote: one rule across documents, auto-cancel at award, a small notice

**Epic** E12 · **M3** · **engine/L** · after **E12-S23**, **E12-S08**, **E13-S07** · tags: `money` (Opus review)
**Requirements:** SRC-009, SRC-018, SRC-007 (sealing), FIN-011 (idempotent posting), TEN-010

> **Status: pre-flight 5 findings folded in 1 Nov 2026 (Opus B: unpriced share kept out of `scope`; one
> lock pass over every affected line; cancel and restore preconditions; rounding); ready for pre-flight 6.**
> Pre-flight against the merged code showed the 628183cd decisions could not run as written (Q1 deadlocks,
> Q2 shrank open quantity twice, Q3 had no mechanism). The operator re-decided Q1 and Q3 and added Q4 and
> Q5; ## Decisions is the only binding version. The notification engine is still E17-S02 (M4); the notice
> uses a no-op hook until then.
> **Why (operator, 28 Oct 2026):** an RFQ line that no vendor priced (not quoted by anyone, or refused as
> a zero price by E12-S23) must not silently vanish. Anyone investigating why an item is not on a PO must
> see why. It is **derived, never stored**, so it cannot drift.

## Rules (operator)

1. **One condition, no new column.** An RFQ line has *no priced quote* when no current submitted bid has
   `bid_line.quoted = true` for it. Since E12-S23 a quoted line always has a price above 0 (DB CHECK
   `bid_line_quoted_price_positive`, migration 0082), and a zero price is refused, so "unquoted by
   everyone" and "refused as zero" are the same condition by construction. One helper decides it,
   everywhere.
2. **Auto-cancel at award approval.** When an award is approved, every requisition line slice sourced by
   an RFQ line with no priced quote is cancelled and its reservation share is released and returned to
   the main project, in the approval transaction, so the requisition can still complete (decision 206).
   The award screen and the approver's decision panel **warn before approval** and name the lines.
3. **A small notice.** The requester is told which lines were cancelled and why, through a no-op hook
   (E17-S02 wires it); the audit entry names the lines.

## Decisions (operator, 31 Oct 2026; replace the 628183cd block)

- **Q1 — cancel before `mark_awarded`.** `mark_awarded` only advances when every line has
  `awarded_qty == quantity - cancelled_qty` (`requisitions/sourcing.py:616-620`), and `cancel_lines` refuses
  a `sourced` requisition (`:366`), so "cancel after `mark_awarded`" can never run. The cancel runs first,
  through a new requisitions function that works while the requisition is `sourced`. `cancel_lines` is
  not changed. No status transition is added or altered: the cancel moves quantities, not status.
- **Q2 — only the unpriced RFQ slice.** The quantity cancelled is the RFQ line's quantity, never the
  requisition line's whole remainder. It moves from `sourced_qty` to `cancelled_qty`, so open quantity
  (`quantity - cancelled - sourced`) is unchanged and the rest of a partly sourced line is untouched.
  Only that slice's reservation share is released.
- **Q3 — a cancel is final.** Cancelled quantity never becomes open again (as `cancel_lines` already
  states: "cannot be ordered again", `:344`). To buy it, the requester raises a new requisition. The
  re-source guard is the existing one, `open = quantity - cancelled - sourced` (`sourcing.py:131`); no
  reinstate path is built, now or as a follow-up.
- **Q4 — supersede restores.** Superseding an approved award (decision 213: "undo exactly") moves each
  auto-cancelled slice back from `cancelled_qty` to `sourced_qty` and returns its funds. Refused once a PO
  exists, as today. A requisition the award left `completed` (every line cancelled) goes back through the
  **existing** transitions: `reopen_for_purchase` (`completed -> open_for_purchase`), then `mark_unawarded`
  (`-> sourced`). `mark_unawarded` is not extended; no state-machine edge is added (operator, pre-flight 2
  finding 2).
- **Q5 — requisition shows the reason after approval only.** Bids are sealed until the deadline (SRC-007)
  and evaluation is not the requester's to see. The requisition shows the reason only on slices an
  approved award cancelled. The RFQ comparison and award screens show it live, to people who may already
  see bids.

- **Q6 — money: refuse at submit a requisition with no sub-project, priced or not** (pre-flight 3).
  A requisition whose every line is unpriced has no award rows, so award submit's sub-project check
  (`sourcing/awards.py:717-729`) skips it; with its reservation on the main project, `release_unawarded`
  moves no funds and writes no `award:{id}:return:{req}` entry (`requisitions/sourcing.py:591`), so
  supersede would never re-reserve it (`:689`). The existing check ("<REQ> has no sub-project; award it
  later.", `standalone_not_supported`) now runs over **every** requisition the award touches: award-row
  requisitions plus those of no-priced-quote RFQ lines. No new ledger key; every release this story
  adds keeps a transfer supersede reverses.
- **Q7 — state: no bid changes while an award awaits approval** (pre-flight 3). The RFQ stays
  `under_evaluation` while its award is `approval_pending` (`awards.py:954`), and a bid the vendor sent
  before the deadline is not late (`bids.py:125`), so it could still be submitted mid-approval and change
  the unpriced set the approver saw. Bid submit and bid replace refuse with 409 `award_pending`, "This
  RFQ's award is awaiting approval. Bids can no longer change." A guard, not a transition: withdrawing or
  rejecting the award (back to draft) lets bids change again. What the approver sees is exactly what is
  cancelled and awarded.

- **Scope — an RFQ no vendor priced at all is not awarded** (pre-flight 4; existing behaviour, no new
  rule). Award submit keeps "Award at least one line" (`awards.py:696`), so auto-cancel only ever runs
  through an approved award with at least one priced line. An RFQ with nothing priced is cancelled by the
  existing RFQ cancel (`sourcing/lifecycle.py:427`, decision 212: history stays, quantity goes back, no
  money moves); the requisition is then re-sourced or withdrawn as today. The comparison Signal still
  shows on every line, which is how the buyer sees why.
- **Award submit re-validates its saved rows** (pre-flight 4; enforces the existing `_store` rule). A
  rejected or withdrawn award returns to `draft` (`awards.py:914-924`) with its rows kept, and bids may
  change in draft (Q7), so a saved row can point at a bid that is no longer current or a line no longer
  quoted. `submit` re-runs the `_store` check on every row against the current bids (shared predicate,
  design 1): the row's bid must be the vendor's current bid and its bid line `quoted`, with quantity
  within the quote. Otherwise the existing `cannot_submit` problem, per row: "The vendor revised this bid
  since the line was chosen. Choose it again." This makes award rows and `no_priced_quote` disjoint at
  submit, and Q7 keeps them so until approval: no RFQ slice can be both awarded and cancelled.

## Design

1. **Helper.** `sourcing.service.no_priced_quote(conn, scope, rfq_id) -> set[UUID]` (RFQ line ids): one
   SQL over the current-bid predicate, `quoted = true`. Move the current-bid predicate into one SQL
   fragment used by the helper, `_current_bids` and `_store` (two copies today). **Leave `_replaced_by`
   alone**: it answers a different question (the direct replacement, for navigation along a chain).
   Comparison's `uncovered_lines` and `LineRow` read from the same set. A test asserts the helper equals
   `compute().uncovered_lines` on the engine fixtures, so the two cannot diverge.
2. **Award, PO, vendor copy (Rule 1).** Already structurally safe (facts table). Add assertions, no code:
   `_store` refuses an award line on a no-priced-quote RFQ line; a PO built from an award has no line for
   it; the vendor copy has none.
3. **Approval money.** `scope`, `over_scope_by` and `assert_within` are **unchanged**: the unpriced share
   never enters `scope`, so it can never cover an over-scope priced award (pre-flight 5). In `figures`,
   compute a separate per-requisition `unpriced` = sum over its no-priced-quote RFQ lines of
   `quantize(ql.amount * rfq_line.quantity / ql.quantity, rfq_ccy)` (the awarded-share formula), and add
   `RequisitionCheck.unpriced`. `release` becomes `max(0, scope - award) + unpriced`. Include requisitions
   whose RFQ lines are all unpriced in the `award_funding` set and in `checks` (`scope` 0, `award` 0,
   `unpriced` > 0; skipped today). The existing single `release_unawarded` call per requisition passes
   that `release` as `unused`, under the existing key, so `restore_unawarded` on supersede reverses all of
   it. **No second `release_unawarded` call and no new ledger key.** The evidence shows `unpriced` beside
   the release.
   **Submit (Q6):** award submit runs the existing sub-project check over the same requisition set (the
   shared helper of design 5), so an all-unpriced requisition without a generated sub-project is refused
   with the existing `standalone_not_supported` problem.
4. **Approval quantities.** New `requisitions.service.cancel_unpriced(conn, scope, award_id,
   changes: Sequence[tuple[UUID, Decimal]], actor_id)` (requisition_line_id, RFQ line quantity), exported
   through `service` (module boundary). Precondition: the requisition is in a pre-award state,
   `sourcing_in_progress` or `sourced` (nothing checks requisition status at award time, so a partly
   sourced requisition can be awarded today); it changes no status. For each change: `sourced_qty -= qty`,
   `cancelled_qty += qty`, refusing if `sourced_qty` would go below 0 or `ordered + cancelled` above
   `quantity`. Moving sourced to cancelled keeps `mark_sourced`'s equality (`sourced == quantity -
   cancelled`) as it was. Audits `requisition.cancel_unpriced.update` per line with before/after
   `sourced_qty`/`cancelled_qty`, the award id, the RFQ line id and reason `no_priced_quote`.
   Called in `_approved` after the award and RFQ locks and status checks (step 6-7) and before
   `mark_awarded`; then `mark_awarded`; then `mark_completed` for each requisition (a no-op unless nothing
   is left to order). Idempotency is the award status check under the award lock: a retried decision
   returns at `settle` or refuses at the locked re-check and the whole transaction rolls back, so nothing
   is cancelled twice.
   **Lock pass (pre-flight 5).** New `requisitions.service.lock_lines(conn, scope, line_ids)`: locks the
   owning requisitions in id order, then the lines `ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE` (the `award_lines` pattern,
   `requisitions/sourcing.py:505-522`). `_approved` and `supersede` each call it **once**, over the union
   of award-row requisition lines and the requisition lines of no-priced-quote RFQ lines, at the point
   where `award_lines` locks today: in `_approved` immediately before `award_lines` (so before the award
   lock), in `supersede` immediately before the `award_lines` reversal (after the award and RFQ locks, as
   today). `award_lines`, `cancel_unpriced` and `restore_unpriced` then re-lock rows the transaction
   already holds (no wait). Every requisition and line is therefore locked in one id-ordered pass, at
   the same position in each path's lock order as today.
   **Rounding.** Each unpriced share uses the existing per-line `quantize` formula, so the release is
   exactly the sum of the shares; when a slice is a line's whole quantity the share is `ql.amount`
   exactly. A sub-cent residual between slices of one line split across RFQs stays in the reservation,
   exactly as it does for awarded shares today (`awards.py:369`); this story does not change it.
5. **Supersede (Q4).** New `requisitions.service.restore_unpriced(conn, scope, award_id, changes,
   actor_id)`, the exact inverse (`cancelled -> sourced`), audited `requisition.restore_unpriced.update`,
   refusing if `cancelled_qty < qty` on a line. Precondition: any state approval can leave the requisition
   in (`sourcing_in_progress`, `sourced`, `open_for_purchase`, `completed`); it changes no status.
   `supersede` derives `changes` from `no_priced_quote` for the award's RFQ. Its requisition set becomes
   award-row requisitions **plus** the requisitions of every no-priced-quote RFQ line (the same set
   approval uses, design 3), so a requisition with no priced line is locked in `award_funding`, gets its
   `restore_unawarded`, and is moved back. Per requisition, in this order: `restore_unpriced` ->
   `restore_unawarded` -> `reopen_for_purchase` -> `mark_unawarded`. `restore_unpriced` lowers
   `cancelled_qty` first, which is what lets the existing reopen guard (`issued < quantity - cancelled`)
   pass on a requisition approval had `completed`; both moves are no-ops for any other state.
   `reopen_for_purchase` gains an optional `reason` (default unchanged, "Issued quantity reduced");
   supersede passes "Award superseded". Use one helper for the set, called by both `_approved` and
   `supersede`, so the two cannot diverge. Re-deriving is exact only
   if the set cannot change while an award is live: bids are immutable once submitted (migration 0071
   triggers), an `awarded` RFQ already refuses bid submit and replace, and Q7 adds the same refusal while
   the award is `approval_pending`: in `_assert_not_awarded` (`bids.py:108`), after the RFQ lock, read the
   RFQ's award status and raise 409 `award_pending` when it is `approval_pending`. Award submit takes the
   RFQ lock first too, so the check cannot race it. After supersede the award is `superseded` and bids
   may change again, which is correct: the restore has already run.
6. **Notice.** No-op `notify_requester_lines_cancelled(conn, scope, requisition_id, line_ids)` in
   `requisitions/sourcing.py`, docstring "E17-S02 wires the requester's notification here, in the award
   approval transaction", called once per requisition with cancelled lines.
7. **UI.** A derived fact is a Signal (MASTER §7.14, `components/ui/Signal.tsx`, `.sig.is-warn`, icon plus
   words, reason text from the server), never a StatusPill and never colour alone.
   - **RFQ comparison, By Line** (`components/sourcing/comparison/Detail.tsx`): Signal "No vendor priced
     this line" on the line row. Backend: `LineRow.no_priced_quote: bool`.
   - **Award screen** (`components/sourcing/award/Lines.tsx`): the empty fieldset for an uncovered line
     becomes the same Signal. `Checks.tsx` adds, beside the existing release sentence: "N lines have no
     priced quote and will be cancelled on approval: <line number, item, quantity>" (singular for one).
     Backend: `AwardRead.unpriced_lines`.
   - **Approver** (`components/approvals/evidence/AwardEvidence.tsx`): the same list, from `evidence()`
     (`awards.py:991-1062`), so it is in the approval snapshot the approver decides on.
   - **Requisition Lines card** (`components/requisitions/DetailCards.tsx`): on a line with
     auto-cancelled slices, **one** Signal per line listing every cancelling RFQ with its quantity, in RFQ
     number order: "Cancelled: no vendor priced this line (RFQ-012: 6, RFQ-015: 2)"; one RFQ reads
     "(RFQ-012: 6)". Only slices from approved awards (Q5). Backend: derived on `LineRead` as
     `unpriced_cancellations: list[{rfq_id, rfq_number, quantity}]` from approved awards' RFQs and
     `no_priced_quote`; nothing stored. Field help on the Cancelled column explains the reason (XFN-008).
   - Placement and copy follow `design-system/pages/rfq-comparison.md` and `requisitions.md`; neither has
     a section for this yet, so record the gap in `notes.followup` for the design session. Tokens only.
8. **Cleanup.** Delete the dead zero guard at `comparison.py:243-248`.

**Lock order (unchanged; convention R, `docs/m2-conventions.md:172-193`).** Approval: approval instance ->
project_balance (id asc) -> requisitions (id asc) -> requisition lines (id asc) -> award -> rfq.
Supersede: project_balance -> award -> rfq -> requisitions (id asc) -> lines (id asc), as today. The
story widens the requisition and line sets (lock pass) and the balance set (`award_funding`) to include
unpriced lines; it adds no new lock type and moves no lock to a new position.

## Acceptance

- [ ] An RFQ line no vendor quoted and one whose only quote was refused as zero are both in
      `no_priced_quote` and show the same Signal text on comparison and award screens.
- [ ] Helper equals `compute().uncovered_lines` on the engine fixtures; the current-bid predicate exists once.
- [ ] Approval cancels exactly the unpriced slices: `cancelled_qty += rfq_line.quantity`, `sourced_qty -=`
      the same; on a line sourced 6 by this RFQ and 4 by another, the other 4 are untouched and open
      quantity is unchanged.
- [ ] Approval releases awarded-unused plus the unpriced share in one posting per requisition, key
      `award:{id}:return:{req}`; the sub-project -> main transfer equals it; balances reconcile.
- [ ] A priced award over a requisition's `scope` is refused by `assert_within` even when that
      requisition also has unpriced lines (the unpriced share never offsets scope).
- [ ] Approval and supersede lock every affected requisition and line in one id-ordered pass, where
      `award_lines` locks today; two-connection test: approval of an award with a fully-unpriced requisition racing a PO
      cancel or `award_lines` on the same requisition does not deadlock.
- [ ] Approval on a `sourcing_in_progress` requisition (a line partly sourced elsewhere) cancels the
      unpriced slice and leaves the status unchanged.
- [ ] An RFQ over two requisitions where one has only unpriced lines: that requisition is cancelled,
      fully released, and ends `completed`; the other proceeds as today.
- [ ] A retried approval decision posts nothing and cancels nothing more; two concurrent approvals on
      real Postgres (two connections) produce exactly one cancel and one posting, no deadlock.
- [ ] Supersede restores `sourced_qty`/`cancelled_qty` and funds exactly; still refused once a PO exists.
- [ ] Supersede of an award over two requisitions, one with only unpriced lines that approval left
      `completed`: that requisition gets its funds back (`restore_unawarded` posts) and ends `sourced` via
      `reopen_for_purchase` then `mark_unawarded`, audited "Award superseded"; `mark_unawarded` still
      refuses `completed` (unchanged).
- [ ] A requisition line cancelled by two approved awards on different RFQs shows one Signal naming
      both RFQs and quantities.
- [ ] Navigation from an older bid revision to its replacement still works (`_replaced_by` unchanged).
- [ ] Bid submit and bid replace (on time or late) return 409 `award_pending` while the RFQ's award is
      `approval_pending`, and succeed again after the award is withdrawn or rejected to draft; a bid
      submit racing award submit on two connections either lands before (and appears in the snapshot)
      or is refused.
- [ ] After an award is rejected to draft and a vendor submits a revision that drops a chosen line,
      award submit refuses that row with `cannot_submit` and writes nothing; re-choosing passes.
- [ ] No approved award has a row whose RFQ line is in `no_priced_quote` (asserted in the approval tests).
- [ ] An RFQ with no priced line cannot be awarded ("Award at least one line"); the existing RFQ cancel
      still returns its quantity with no money moved.
- [ ] Award submit with an all-unpriced requisition that has no generated sub-project returns the
      existing `standalone_not_supported` problem naming it; nothing is written.
- [ ] Award screen and decision panel name the lines before approval; the approval snapshot carries them.
- [ ] No stored flag: before the deadline, a vendor submitting a revision that quotes the line removes it
      from `no_priced_quote` on the next read.
- [ ] Sealed: before the deadline, comparison stays sealed and the requisition shows no reason; after
      approval the requisition shows the reason on the cancelled slice only.
- [ ] Requester notice hook called once per affected requisition with the cancelled line ids; audit rows
      name award, RFQ line and reason.
- [ ] Re-sourcing a cancelled slice is refused by the existing `over_open_quantity` guard (test).
- [ ] TEN-010: no new routes; the comparison, award and requisition-lines foreign-tenant tests cover the
      new fields. Authorization per role on the award approve path unchanged (test).
- [ ] Playwright happy path, axe and a keyboard-only path on the comparison and award screens; MASTER §8
      checklist; `flo check --light` green.
