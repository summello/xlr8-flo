## Question

Sonnet has 29 merged stories, **1.72 mean rounds, 59% clean**, against Codex at 1.04 and 97%. `policy.author_priority` is `[sonnet, opencode-nemotron, codex]`, so the weakest measured author is tried first. Is that churn concentrated in specific kinds or tags, or spread evenly? And is the number evidence of Sonnet's quality at all?

## Pre-flight evidence (Opus, 22 Oct 2026, computed from `agents/scorecard.json` at `799797d`)

Reproduce before trusting; the story must regenerate every number below from the file.

**Sonnet, all 29** (first record is `E09-S06`, index 76 of 106)

| Slice | n | mean rounds | clean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gated (any of money, auth, security, migration, concurrency) | 12 | 2.17 | 33% |
| ungated | 17 | 1.41 | 76% |

By kind: ledger 2.67 (n=3), api 3.0 (n=1), security 2.5 (n=2), engine 1.86 (n=7), ui 1.62 (n=8), crud 1.0 (n=4), chore 1.0 (n=3), test 1.0 (n=1).
By tag (a story counts once per tag): migration 3.0 (n=4), concurrency 2.5 (2), auth 2.33 (6), money 2.25 (8), security 2.0 (3), ui 1.75 (12), none 1.25 (8).
Rounds distribution: seventeen 1s, five 2s, five 3s, two 4s. `gate_failures` 0 and `escapes` 0 across all 29.

**On its face this reads as "concentrated in gated work".** Four confounds stop that from being a conclusion:

1. **There is no like-for-like Codex comparison.** Of the 30 records since Sonnet's first, 29 are Sonnet and 1 is Codex. Codex's 1.04 (67 stories, 50 of them gated) comes from M0 and M1, when review was Opus alone (25 Aug and 10 Oct arrangements) and many stories were foundations written under a sole-author contract. Since 13 Oct every Sonnet story is reviewed by Codex, and gated ones by a fresh Opus too. **Author and review regime are fully confounded.**
2. **Findings and rounds are anti-correlated in the data.** Sonnet stories with `rounds = 1` hold 15 findings and 13 blockers; stories with `rounds >= 2` hold **0** findings and 0 blockers. A blocker in a one-round story is not possible under the stated round definition, so `review` in the record reflects something other than what drove `rounds` (likely the last verdict only). **The scorecard cannot currently say why a story took a second round.**
3. **Not every round is the author's.** `project-memory.md` (22 Oct) records three Codex reviews that hit the command cap and left `approve, ran_tests: false`; each cost a second review. If `rounds` counts those, reviewer failures are being scored against the author.
4. **Small cells.** Most kind and tag cells have n < 8, several have n = 1.

## Decision rule (mechanical, so the finding is not a mood)

Compute over Sonnet's records, gated vs ungated as defined above.

- **Concentrated** if `gated mean - ungated mean >= 0.5` and gated n >= 8. (Preliminary: 0.76 and 12, so concentrated.)
- **Spread** otherwise.

Then apply the confound checks. The recommendation depends on both:

| Rule result | Confounds 1 to 3 resolved? | Recommend |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrated | yes | Add `avoid_tags: [money, auth, security, migration, concurrency]` to Sonnet in `agents.yaml` (gated work goes to Codex); E23-S04 ships its reorder as scoped for the ungated lane |
| Concentrated | **no (expected)** | **Do not narrow Sonnet yet, and do not run a Codex A/B yet.** Wait for 10 verified Sonnet gated records and judge then (Judgement day in the finding doc). E23-S04 ships the reorder for ungated work only. (An earlier version proposed an A/B "under the same Codex-plus-Opus review"; that was wrong: Codex cannot review its own stories, so the arms would have different non-Opus reviewers.) |
| Spread | any | E23-S04 proceeds as scoped; E23-S05 (pre-flight) is the likely fix |

A recommendation that narrows Sonnet while confound 1 is open is a defect in this story: it would send more gated work to Codex, whose weekly budget is `weekly_story_budget: 18`, on evidence that cannot tell author quality from review strictness.
