# E13-S23 — PO approval withdrawal: approval instance before PO

**Epic** E13 · **M3** · **concurrency/S** · after **E13-S07** · tags: `concurrency` (Opus review)

> Found by Codex pre-flight 12 of E13-S07 (30 Oct 2026). Raised by the operator as its own story.

## Requirements (inlined)

- **PO-007** — a PO that needs approval goes through the approval workflow; its creator may withdraw it while pending.

## Problem

`PurchaseOrderService.withdraw` (`apps/api/src/flo/modules/purchasing/service.py:859-876`) locks the PO
(`self._row(id, lock=True)`) and **then** calls `approvals.service.withdraw`, which locks
`approval_instance` (`approvals/decisions.py:362`). A final approval decision takes the opposite order:
`approval_instance` first (`decisions.py:226`), then the outcome adapter locks the PO
(`purchasing/service.py:880`). A creator withdrawing while an approver decides the same PO can deadlock;
Postgres aborts one side with 40P01, which reaches the client as a 500.

## Design

Copy `ChangeService.withdraw` (`purchasing/changes.py:545-569`): read the PO **unlocked**, check
creator-only and `status == 'approval_pending'` (409 `invalid_transition`) on that read, then call
`withdraw(document_type='po', ...)` holding no PO lock. The adapter's `settle` locks the PO and moves it
to `draft` under the instance lock, so the order is instance → PO on every path. A decision that
finishes first makes `withdraw` raise 409 `task_closed`; let it through unchanged. No API, schema or
migration change.

## Acceptance

- [ ] `withdraw` takes no PO lock before `approvals.service.withdraw` (unit test asserting no `FOR UPDATE` on `purchase_order` before the instance lock, or a code-level assertion the reviewer can read).
- [ ] **Concurrency (real Postgres):** withdraw and a final approve racing on one PO, many iterations: no 40P01; exactly one of PO `draft` (withdrawn) or `approved`, the loser 409 `task_closed` or `invalid_transition`.
- [ ] Existing withdraw tests still green (creator-only 403, non-pending 409).

## Plants

1. Restore `self._row(id, lock=True)` before `withdraw`: the concurrency test sees a deadlock and fails.
