# E12-S05 — Sealed bids and the late-bid block with an audited override

**Epic** E12 · **M3** · **security/M** · after **E12-S04** · tags: `security`, `auth` (Opus review)

> **Decisions:** canvas DECISIONS 108 and 118 (a sealed round is hidden from **everyone**, evaluators
> and the RFQ owner included), 145 (the vendor's sent time decides lateness), 146 (the lead,

> **Writes:** every `PATCH`/`PUT`/`DELETE` on an editable resource follows `docs/m2-conventions.md` §O (`If-Match` with the integer version, `ETag` on reads, 400 when missing, 409 `stale_version` on mismatch). `Idempotency-Key` protects **POST** commands only (the middleware ignores other methods); where a packet says "(Idempotency-Key)" on a non-POST route, read §O instead.

## Requirements (inlined)

- **SRC-007** — Bids must remain confidential from other vendors and from unauthorized internal users.
- **SRC-008** — Late bids must be blocked or explicitly marked and accepted only through an audited override.

## Sealed

**Before `rfq.deadline_at`, no bid figure leaves the server for anyone, any role.** This covers
prices, tax, freight, totals, lead time, validity, terms, requirement answers, counts of met
requirements, and evaluator scores (E12-S07 calls the same guard). What may leave: which invitees have
a submitted bid (`3 Received, sealed`) and their `sent_at` and `keyed_at`.

One function, `sourcing.sealing.visible(rfq, now) -> bool`, is called by **every** read path that
returns bid content: the bids list, bid detail, the comparison (E12-S06), evaluation (E12-S07),
exports, and the link-graph node. Sealed responses use a separate schema
(`SealedBidRead: vendor, sent_at, keyed_at, status`) **with no optional figure fields**, so a sealed
response cannot carry a figure by accident.

**The keyer of a sealed bid** sees the fields they typed **only while the bid is a draft**. Once it
is submitted, they are sealed out too (decision 118: hidden from everyone).

After the deadline, the read authorization E12-S04 shipped is unchanged: `rfq.approve` on the
RFQ's unit reads every bid. `list_bids` needs `rfq.approve` or `rfq.edit` (without `rfq.approve` it
returns only the bids the caller keyed); `get_bid` needs only membership and, without `rfq.approve`,
returns the bid only to its keyer (`keyed_by`, else 403) — a keyer with neither `rfq.edit` nor
`rfq.approve` still reads their own bid (`test_bids.py` around line 413 asserts it; keep it). This story does **not** widen reads to `rfq.view`;
evaluators read through E12-S07.

### One presenter, every response (pre-flight, 24 Oct 2026)

- `BidService._present(deadline_passed: bool, row) -> BidRead | SealedBidRead`: a draft returns
  `BidRead` **only when `row.keyed_by == self.actor_id`**; a draft keyed by anyone else returns
  `SealedBidRead`, before **and** after the deadline (Decision, draft visibility = A: `rfq.approve`'s
  `all_bids` list shows another keyer's draft as a status row only). Any non-draft returns
  `SealedBidRead` unless `sealing.visible(...)`. **Every** method that returns a bid goes through it: `list_bids`,
  `read`, `capture`, `patch`, `submit` (its POST response is sealed the moment it submits, before
  the deadline) and `replace`. `_read` stays the private builder of the full shape and is called only
  by `_present` and by E12-S06/S07 after their own `visible` check.
- `SealedBidRead` fields: `id, rfq_id, vendor_id, keyed_by, status, sent_at, keyed_at,
  submitted_at, replaces_bid_id, replaced_by_bid_id, row_version, late` (no figure, no line, no answer, no
  `evidence_attachment_id`). `extra="forbid"`.
- `api/sourcing.py`: `response_model=BidRead | SealedBidRead` on `get_bid`, `create_bid`,
  `patch_bid`, `submit_bid` and `replace_bid`, and `list[BidRead | SealedBidRead]` on `list_bids`;
  `_bid_done` takes either (both carry `row_version` for the `ETag`). Regenerate the web client.

### Evidence attachments and notes on a bid (pre-flight, 24 Oct 2026)

A bid's evidence file is the vendor's quote, so it is bid content. Today it leaks: the bid's
`DocumentAccess` in `modules/sourcing/service.py::register_access()` only guards **writes**
(`bid_attachments_open`), and `tests/sourcing/test_bids.py` (around line 474) asserts a submitted
bid's attachment stays readable with `rfq.approve` + `record.read`. Seal it in the existing registry,
no new route:

1. `modules/collaboration/service.py`: add `read_guard: Callable[[Conn, Scope, UUID, IdentityId], None] | None = None`
   to `DocumentAccess` (same shape as `write_authorize`, which already takes the actor), and in
   `CollaborationService.authorize` call it as `read_guard(self.connection, self.scope, document_id, self.actor_id)`
   when `not write`, **after** the permission check (so an unpermitted caller still gets 403/404, never
   a hint that the bid exists or is sealed). Every read that goes through `authorize` is then covered:
   `GET /api/v1/documents/bid/{id}/attachments`, `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}/content` (it authorizes
   via `linked_document`), the attachment-version and revision reads, and the bid's notes list.
   `attachments_unchecked`/`notes_unchecked` are not called for `bid` anywhere today; keep it so.
2. `modules/sourcing/service.py`: add `bid_attachments_sealed(connection, scope, id, actor_id)` beside
   `bid_attachments_open`. It selects `b.status, b.keyed_by, r.deadline_at` (bid joined to its RFQ,
   `org_id` scoped) and calls `sealing.visible(...)`; a draft passes **only for its keyer**
   (`keyed_by == actor_id`; anyone else gets 409 `bid_sealed`, before and after the deadline — Decision A);
   any other status before the deadline raises `conflict("bid_sealed", "Bids are sealed until the
   bidding stop time.")` (409, `checks.problem = "bid_sealed"`). Register it as `read_guard=` on the
   `bid` `DocumentAccess`. Writes keep `bid_attachments_open` unchanged.
3. Update the existing assertion in `tests/sourcing/test_bids.py` that a submitted bid's
   attachment reads stay open: before the deadline it is now 409 `bid_sealed`; after the deadline
   (move `rfq.deadline_at` into the past in the fixture) the permission ladder it already asserts
   (`record.read` alone 403, `rfq.approve` alone 403, both 200) holds unchanged.

## Late bids

In `check_late(bid)` (the hook E12-S04 calls at submit): `bid.sent_at > rfq.deadline_at` (the
vendor's time, decision 145; never `keyed_at`) → 409 `late_bid`, unless the body carries
`accept_late_reason` (1..500) **and** the caller holds `rfq.approve` on the RFQ's unit. The caller
is still the bid's keyer (`_own_draft`) and passes the route's `rfq.edit` check, so the override is
exercised by a lead who keys the bid. The router computes it: in `submit_bid`, `may_accept_late =
permits(request, "rfq.approve", rfq_target(id, connection))` (add `request: Request`), passed as
`BidService.submit(..., may_accept_late=...)` and on to `_before_submit(rfq_row, bid_row, body,
may_accept_late)`. A reason without the permission is 403; the service never reads permissions itself. An accepted
late bid stores `late_accepted_by`, `late_accepted_at` and `late_reason`, is audited
(`bid.late_override`), and every read returns `late: {accepted_by_email, at, reason}`. A bid keyed
after the deadline but **sent** before it is on time.

**Out of scope:** the comparison and evaluation themselves (they must call `visible`), and the UI.

**From E12-S04 (pre-flight, 23 Oct 2026):** E12-S04 does not call `check_late`; it leaves the seam `BidService._before_submit(rfq_row, bid_row, body)` (a no-op inside the submit lock) and an empty `BidSubmit` (`extra="forbid"`). This story fills the seam, adds `accept_late_reason` to `BidSubmit`, and adds the `late_*` columns in its own migration. Bids are immutable after submit (`bid_guard`), so the late columns are written in the same UPDATE that sets `status = 'submitted'`: change the seam's return type to `dict[str, object]` (the extra `SET` values: `{}` when on time, else `late_accepted_by`, `late_accepted_at = now()`, `late_reason`), and have `submit` append those columns to its existing UPDATE (fixed column names, values as bound parameters, never interpolated). Lateness is decided in SQL under the RFQ lock already taken (`bid.sent_at > rfq.deadline_at`: append `deadline_at` as the third column of `_lock_rfq`'s SELECT, leaving indexes 0 and 1 as they are, and compare it with the locked bid row's `sent_at`, `row[5]`), not against the application clock. The `bid.late_override` audit is written in the same transaction, after the UPDATE, beside `bid.submit`. `replace` (E12-S04 Decision 2) creates a draft; that draft's own submit passes through this same seam, so a late replacement is judged on its own `sent_at` with no extra code.

## Decision (operator, 24 Oct 2026; was Blocked)

**Q1 = A. A replacement copies structure only.** `BidService.replace` (E12-S04, merged; changed in
this story) still inserts the new draft with `vendor_id`, `rfq_version`, `currency`, the new
`sent_at`, `keyed_by` = the caller and `replaces_bid_id`, but **no figure, term or answer**:
- `bid`: `freight` takes its column default `0`; `lead_time_days`, `valid_until`, `delivery_terms`,
  `warranty`, `exceptions` are `NULL`; `evidence_attachment_id` `NULL` (as today).
- `bid_line`: one row per original line with `rfq_line_id` and `quantity` copied, `quoted = false`,
  `unit_price = NULL`, `line_total = NULL`, `tax` its default `0` (satisfies the
  `quoted = (unit_price IS NOT NULL) AND quoted = (line_total IS NOT NULL)` check in
  `migrations/20261022_0071_bids.py`).
- `bid_requirement`: **not copied**; the keyer answers every requirement again.
The keyer re-keys from the vendor's corrected quote; the existing replace audit row is unchanged.

**Q2 = A. Submit only in a live bidding state.** In `BidService.submit`, right after `_lock_rfq`
and before any other check, on the locked row's `status` (index 0): `awarded` → 409 `rfq_awarded`
(call the existing `_assert_not_awarded`); `cancelled` → 409 `rfq_cancelled` ("This RFQ is
cancelled. Its bids can no longer change."); `draft` → 409 `rfq_not_published` ("This RFQ is not
published yet."). `published`, `closed` and `under_evaluation` proceed. This applies to every
submit, late or not; the late check in `_before_submit` comes after it.

## Decision (operator, 24 Oct 2026): draft visibility = A

A draft bid is visible in full only to its keyer (`keyed_by`). Holders of `rfq.approve` see other
keyers' drafts as a status row with no figures (no prices, tax, freight, terms, answers) —
`SealedBidRead`. Full figures reach `rfq.approve` only once a bid is submitted **and** the deadline
has passed. Implemented by `_present` (Sealed, above) and tested by the draft-visibility acceptance
criterion and plant 4. The same rule covers the draft's evidence attachment and notes: their read
guard admits a draft's attachment only to its keyer (409 `bid_sealed` for anyone else).
