# E12-S05 — Sealed bids and the late-bid block with an audited override

**Epic** E12 · **M3** · **security/M** · after **E12-S04** · tags: `security`, `auth` (Opus review)

> **Conventions:** `docs/m1-conventions.md`, `docs/m2-conventions.md`, `docs/author-traps.md`.
> **Decisions:** canvas DECISIONS 108 and 118 (a sealed round is hidden from **everyone**, evaluators
> and the RFQ owner included), 145 (the vendor's sent time decides lateness), 146 (the lead,
> `rfq.approve` on the unit, may accept a late bid with a reason). `design-system/pages/sourcing.md`
> §2 "Sealed means sealed", `pages/rfq-comparison.md` §2. Back end only.

## Requirements (inlined)

- **SRC-007** — Bids must remain confidential from other vendors and from unauthorized internal users.
- **SRC-008** — Late bids must be blocked or explicitly marked and accepted only through an audited override.

## Sealed

**Before `rfq.deadline_at`, no bid figure leaves the server for anyone, any role.** This covers
prices, tax, freight, totals, lead time, validity, terms, requirement answers, counts of met
requirements, and evaluator scores (E12-S07 calls the same guard). What may leave: which invitees have
a submitted bid (`3 Received, sealed`) and their `sent_at` and `keyed_at`.

One function, `sourcing.sealing.visible(rfq, now) -> bool`, is called by **every** read path that
returns bid content: the bids list, bid detail, the comparison (E12-S06), evaluation (E12-S07),
exports, and the link-graph node. Sealed responses use a separate schema
(`SealedBidRead: vendor, sent_at, keyed_at, status`) **with no optional figure fields**, so a sealed
response cannot carry a figure by accident.

**The keyer of a sealed bid** sees the fields they typed **only while the bid is a draft**. Once it
is submitted, they are sealed out too (decision 118: hidden from everyone).

After the deadline, `rfq.view` holders on the unit read bids, and evaluators read them through
E12-S07.

## Late bids

In `check_late(bid)` (the hook E12-S04 calls at submit): `bid.sent_at > rfq.deadline_at` (the
vendor's time, decision 145; never `keyed_at`) → 409 `late_bid`, unless the body carries
`accept_late_reason` (1..500) **and** the caller holds `rfq.approve` on the RFQ's unit. An accepted
late bid stores `late_accepted_by`, `late_accepted_at` and `late_reason`, is audited
(`bid.late_override`), and every read returns `late: {accepted_by_email, at, reason}`. A bid keyed
after the deadline but **sent** before it is on time.

## Acceptance

- [ ] **Sealed-bid test, every role, every route, with a planted violation:** seed two submitted bids with distinctive figures (e.g. `98765.4321`). As each role (requestor, sourcing-specialist, the RFQ owner, an evaluator, org admin, the keyer), before the deadline, call every bid-reading route and assert none of those figures (as string, with and without thousands separators) appears in any response body. Then plant a `unit_price` into `SealedBidRead`'s builder: the test must fail.
- [ ] After the deadline, the same call returns the figures to `rfq.view` holders.
- [ ] Late: sent after the deadline → 409; with a reason but without `rfq.approve` → 403; with both → accepted, audited, and marked on every read. Sent before, keyed after → on time.
- [ ] TEN-010 on every bid route.
- [ ] `flo check` green.

## Plants

1. Return figures to the RFQ owner before the deadline: the sealed test fails.
2. Use `keyed_at` for lateness: the keyed-after test fails.
